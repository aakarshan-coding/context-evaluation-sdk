# E-Bike Battery Care and Safety

Lithium-ion batteries are the most expensive part of an e-bike, and how you charge and store them makes a big difference in how long they last. This guide applies to the Metro-E and Metro-E Cargo batteries. Batteries from other brands may have different recommendations, so check your owner's manual.

## Everyday charging

You don't need to run the battery down before charging it. Lithium-ion batteries don't have a memory effect, and shallow charges are easier on the cells than deep ones. If you ride every day, plug in whenever it's convenient.

Charge indoors at room temperature, between 10°C and 30°C (50°F to 86°F). If the battery has been out in the cold, let it warm up inside for an hour before you charge it. Charging a battery below freezing can permanently damage the cells.

Always use the charger that came with your bike. The Metro-E charger is 4 A and the Metro-E Cargo charger is 5 A. They look similar, but they are not interchangeable, and using the wrong one voids the electrical warranty.

Unplug the charger when the battery is full. The battery management system stops charging at 100%, but leaving it on the charger for days at a time is still not recommended.

## Riding in cold and heat

In cold weather, expect up to 30% less range. The cells deliver less energy when cold, but the capacity comes back once the battery warms up. If you store your bike in an unheated garage in winter, bring the battery inside between rides.

Avoid leaving the bike or battery in direct sun in a parked car in summer. Temperatures above 45°C (113°F) speed up aging.

## Long-term storage

If you won't ride for more than a month, store the battery at about 50% charge, which is two or three bars on the display. Keep it indoors somewhere cool and dry, ideally between 10°C and 20°C. Check it every two months and top it back up to about 50% if it has dropped below one bar.

Never store a battery fully empty for long periods. A battery left completely discharged for several months can drop below the voltage the battery management system can recover from, and it will need to be replaced. That is not covered by warranty.

## How long a battery lasts

With normal use, a Metro-E battery keeps about 80% of its original capacity after 500 to 800 full charge cycles. A full cycle is the equivalent of charging from empty to full once, so two charges from 50% to 100% count as one cycle. Most riders reach that point after three to five years.

The display shows battery health under Settings, then Battery. If health drops below 70% within the warranty period, bring the bike in for a warranty check.

## Replacement and recycling

A replacement Metro-E battery is $649 and a replacement Metro-E Cargo battery is $799. Batteries are usually in stock at Boulder; Fort Collins can order one in two business days.

Never put a lithium-ion battery in household trash or recycling. Bring old e-bike batteries, from any brand, to either store and we'll recycle them for free through Call2Recycle.

## Damage and safety

If a battery has been dropped hard, has a cracked case, gets hot while not charging, smells unusual, or swells, stop using it right away. Store it outside, away from anything flammable, and call us. Don't try to open or repair a battery yourself.

Don't pressure wash the battery or the motor, and don't submerge either one. Riding in rain is fine. After a wet ride, dry the battery contacts before charging.

When flying, you can't bring an e-bike battery on a commercial airplane, checked or carried on, because it is well over the 160 Wh airline limit.
